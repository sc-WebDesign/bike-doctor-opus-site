Tell me what it is doing

You don't need to know what is wrong with it

Most people ring up apologising for not knowing the right words. You don't need them. Describe the noise, or when it happens, or what it feels like through the pedals, and that is usually enough to know what I am turning up with.

Here are the ones that come up most. They are not diagnoses, because nobody can diagnose a bike down the phone. They are just the usual suspects, so you know the sort of thing you are describing.

Describe it on the phone
Usual suspects Not a diagnosis Clicking as you pedal Very often the gears rather than anything serious. Cables stretch and the indexing drifts. Squealing brakes Usually pads, alignment, or a rim that needs cleaning. Rarely the expensive answer. Tyre keeps going down Worth checking whether whatever caused it is still in the tyre. Otherwise the new tube goes the same way. It just feels slow Nine times in ten this is tyre pressure and a dry chain, and it is the cheapest thing on this list. Gears won't stay put Indexing, a tired cable, or a bent hanger from the bike going over. Quick to look at, quick to know. If it is none of these, ring anyway. Half the job is working out what you are actually describing.
The work

What I fix, at your door

Everything below happens on site. The van-and-workshop question only comes up for the rare job that genuinely needs a bench, and you get told that before anything is taken apart.

Punctures and tyres

The most common call by a distance. Tube, tyre, rim tape, and a look at what caused it so it does not happen again next week.

Gears and cables

Indexing, new inner and outer cables, mechs adjusted. This is the job that makes an old bike feel like it has been replaced.

Brakes

Pads, alignment and cable tension on rim brakes and discs. Nothing on a bike matters more, and it is usually a quick job.

Servicing

The full going-over: drivetrain cleaned, everything checked and adjusted, and a straight answer about what is worn and what will do another season.

E-bikes

Punctures, brakes and servicing on e-bikes, which are heavy enough that getting one to a shop is the whole problem. Ring first with the make so I turn up right.

Bikes bought online

A boxed bike is not a built bike. Assembly, gears set up properly and a safety check, so the first ride is not the shakedown ride.

No prices are shown here, because a puncture and a full service are not the same job and quoting blind helps nobody. You get told what it is likely to be when you ring, and told again before anything is done.
